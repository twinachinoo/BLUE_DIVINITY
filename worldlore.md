## **Locations**

There are a few important locations to take note of. Reasonable to assume every PC knows.

1\. **Min**. Min is a southern trade city, built on a convergence of Rivers. Built on an endless maze of canals, Min attracts poets and profit alike, their most prized cultural exports being drama, poetry, and art.

Min is sheltered with a lattice of hanging gardens, of flowers that bloom in each season, and lanterns that rise into the sky at night. There is never a day without beauty in this city, and never a night without song.

2\. **Thysqu**: an isolated community of mystics far isolated in the western mountains, Thysqu does not refer to a singular city, but a collective of ancient buildings spread across the top of the mountains. Beyond the clouds, Thysqu is painted with a cold, dark blue, a strange color that seems to seep from within the rivers and rocks themselves. Some say this is what gives the dwellers their psionic powers, others say it’s simply a cosmic influence from being so close to the planes. Whilst the latter is true, it also places them closer to whatever lurks beyond the celestial dome. 

thysqu's mystics often serve as enigmatic oracles and soothsayers for the royal family and the noble houses. Their readings are unerringly true, as they claim the stars have long revealed to them whatever it is they wish to see. Celestial instruments across the continent are tuned to Thysqu.

3\. **Ru Hakh**. “Dome of the Ru”. An isolated, military bastion on the far north border of the empire. A sprawling maze of tall walls, taller towers, and endless soldiers. The city is constructed with a depressing black basalt, weathered from the bitter cold and the lack of care, yet unerringly standing to kill any hope of escape. 

Ru Hakh can barely even be considered a prison \- not for the most violent, but for the most careless. Political prisoners are sent here to starve, and so are innocent commoners, caught in the conflicts of the former. In Ru Hakh, survival is merely a gamble of chance \- harsh winters, food shortages, internal conflict, and exhaustive labour. To survive Ru Hakh, is to earn a badge of honour and shame alike. 

Even in such a lawless and violent prison, Power nonetheless is held in the icy, unfeeling hearts of house Ru Ning, whose decrees are as impossible to move as their frozen will. They’ve designed the prison city to keep secrets as well as people, where daring, volatile truths wither in the cold. 

4\. **Re nu**: Re nu is the imperial great palace city, situated on a set of floating islands far above the peasants below. Great waterfalls descend from each side, as the city is suspended by a network of obsidian obelisks constructed from a bygone age.

Re Nu is nearly entirely powered by a unique convergence of ley lines below, whose ambient magic levitates a shattered landmass above, and conveniently \- also powers any arcane energy expenditure, which there is plenty of. Soon becoming a tourist attraction for researchers and wealthy nobles alike, Re Nu was pleased to expand its elaborate architecture to where it now hosts an entire City within. 

Nobody knows who built the obelisks. or who suspended a landmass this high above the sky. The empire claims it's a work of their greatest mages, but ask any of them, and you get the same response  \- do they not know, or do they not want to know?

5\. **Lhosung**: a city lost to time, buried under the desert that creeps from the southwest. Once a city of gold, a capital of a bygone desert dynasty, now reduced to nomadic tents along a trade route. However, its historic dominance still holds firm despite the sands of time, with grand, exotic architecture beneath the sand dunes. What were grand palaces and great cathedrals \- still reaching out above the sands with their spires. Looming structures and enigmatic carvings still stand unyielding, their stature as solid as the carved stone.

If only for the mysterious knowledge and culture that vanished with the desert dynasty, Lhosung would be nothing more than a scholar’s vacation spot. However, it lays in the epicenter of the supply of *Mornikhor*, or, in common, “Desert gold” \- a rare, sweet-smelling mineral of black and gold color that holds the current key to alchemy. The locals guard their supply fiercely, making extraction attempts difficult at best. Though, as the relentless march of the dunes cross their homes, it’s unknown how long their buried treasure will keep them around.

6\. **Jinhai**: a community of distinguished scholars that grew into a small town. Built around a mysterious crater in the western border, with geometric patterns across the crash site, a lack of flora growth, and the most pressing mystery of all \- how could something this big come down and make no disturbance? there were no reports of any loud sounds, any bright falling objects. No injuries are recorded. 

Jinhai has thus developed into a town of the most distinguished scholars. Who, without the constraints of city planning, could bring their most glorious technologies. Towers of brass and bronze, airships soaring across skynets, great gears and water mills turning, and the eternal smell of ozone and steam. For some, it’s nothing but loud, chaotic and messy, and for others, an artificer’s dream. Clockwork-and-cog drive the city’s every move and moment, from mechanical guards that parade the gates, to the buzzing homunculi that brew morning teas, to the rickety trains that skate across pedestrians without a care. 

7\. **Yisu**: a collection of islands far into the southern sea, covered in maples with golden leaves year round. Rarely do humans make the treacherous journey to reach this green paradise. 

There are ruins of a bygone civilization. Ruins of great stone constructions and intricate sculpture. From what seem like ritual sites, community wells, noble housing \- it was a full world, now left to the decay of time. researchers come and go, but each effort the empire makes to settle these islands leads to the mysterious disappearance of the entire community.

8\. **Latis city**: Adorned with marble towers and palaces above ground, a blatant facade over an underground of poverty and crime. Latis city isn't somewhere to stay for long. It's a city built for merchants, one for goods to dock and flow through trade networks, or take a gander across the famous Sescet district with its operas and elegant boulevards. Whilst money is the big talk above ground, information is the real currency being exchanged below tables.

Amongst those in the know, it's grown infamous because of it. Yet the city never lost the allure of its intel; but rather, it acts as a guise, a tourist destination, that filters out who's here just for gossip, and who's got something worth exchanging. Currently, the City is mainly handled by House Atani, who established their own manor, though the other houses sliced up the pie as they wished.

# **The noble houses**

There are plenty of noble houses that dot the continent, but in the modern era, only 8 have managed to keep their seat in the royal palace. Through cutthroat competition, shady strategy, and ruthless domination, only 8 noble houses have distinguished themselves throughout time.

known collectively as the noble 8, each house has an iron grip over a sector of commerce. However, whilst their guise is business, their true influence stretches far into the grey areas of jurisdiction and governance. Through a mix of bureaucratic negligence, bribery and nepotism, and an inefficient and incompetent emperor, the houses have found their way into wherever they can find an inch of a power vacuum.

Not only is their influence expanding, so is their territorial control. It's rare not to see an office for each of the houses in each major city, especially those focused on trade. Each office is elegantly decorated, with clerks with seamless smiles, filled with paintings, sculptures and ceramics. They claim they sponsor brilliant artists to pretend they're not just ruthless businessmen. and it works. 

Of course, not all houses are equal in their greed or control, and the same can be said for those that work within them.

### **House Atani: maritime trade & Raw goods**

Atani, birthed from a collective of disillusioned sailors and their captains, became the thing they hated. They began by undercutting the luxury ocean goods market, offering fishermen a chance to sell their produce, whilst creating cheap passage through turbulent oceans. 

However, as people flocked to them as customers and employees alike, their sheer size and dominance expanded them well across the east coast, granting unparalleled control over maritime operation. As greed began to seep through the ranks, Atani regularly hiked taxes, imposed strict regulations, and squeezed out small businesses. They now effectively govern all sea access on the east coast, holding a scrying eye over the seas, which are notoriously difficult to divide up control of.

Alcazy has access to the most precise instruments, the purest ingredients, and the best scholars, gathered from generations of being prided as the best house for scholars of medicine. talent funnelled to the top, starving out local jobs and ruining any chance of competition. perhaps the least directly competitive of the houses, choosing intellectual stratification rather than direct competition; but don't be fooled, if there's a coin to be made \- and that coin comes from illegal drugs, then you can be sure Alcazy has a hand in it. # **The Star-Fallen Races**

The origin of a Fallen is unknown, and so is their purpose. They come from afar \- beyond the sky, some say plainly. And yet, through coincidence or purpose, appear uncannily humanoid. Starborns are timeless, ageless, and live for millennia. They appear as one age eternally, which seems to be completely arbitrary. 

The age they appear as, the physique they have, the gender they assign themselves, and other external factors seem but decorative to them. They are pigmented simply, having a pure-white outer appearance, glowing with soft bioluminescence. They appear this way from their hair to their skin, to even their blood. With the occasional highlight of iridescence or pearlescence, they appear as nothing but beings of pure light.

That much is the only similarity between the two main subraces of the Fallen. One, simply titled the Starborn, and an enigmatic, secondary offshoot of them, known as the *seek,* named aptly after their strange nature to constantly seek something from this world. The line between the two is often blurry at best, so it’s not impossible to see a starborn have some elements of their seek cousin, or vice versa.

## **The starborn**

“Pale angels” \- *(Lynyin, “Annals of the Li”)*

What is a starborn? A great question. If you ask one, they will have no answer for you, for even they do not know. Starborn seem to come from anywhere across the cosmos, though you will find them most commonly from the remnants of fallen palestars. 

Starborns appear as humanoid creatures, unique in their muted colors. Most starborns are nothing but crystalline structures of glassy strands inside, covered by a delicate, soft husk. Their exterior appears as soft-white skin, hair, and eyes, each glowing faintly with a faint, celestial light. Some will have some pigment, usually in the hair and eyes, of shades of silver; Even rarer starborns do exist with unique, anomalous physical appearances.

Though, what is most interesting about starborns, is they do not have a specific, defining categorical characteristic. They are not distinctly similar to humans, or elves, or dwarves, or anything else, and what distinctive features they have seem to be genuinely arbitrary. They do not appear to age, nor in fact \- change physically, at all, throughout their lifespans. That may be one of the reasons why they received the title of the pale angel \- retaining eternal beauty and youth, and their pristine white exterior across eons.

It’s understandable why they were given that name, for there were once myths of the stars as deities that could birth spawn. Starborn seems to be more harmonized, synchronized, on some primal level, with the celestial cycles of the world. They will walk pilgrimages to borders between planes. They may sleep in the morning, and wake only under moonlight. They will seek knowledge of the cosmos throughout their lifetime. Perhaps it is a place of their genesis and their terminus, a path sometimes walked across cosmic eons of time

In truth, this is one of the main curiosities of many starborn. And in a way, it reflects their nature. Starborns \- Regardless of their age, history, or personality, seem to share a common trait: an otherworldly nature of serenity and silence. They speak in soft, choral tones, have faces that rarely rage or fear, and seem to drift more than they walk. Many will seek further solace; Some vanish to explore the edge of civilisation, others delve into a century of academia, and others still devote a life to their brush or blade alike. Some even choose to dedicate this to their own mind, being one of the few species capable of psionic power. Some hold it as a prestigious title, others \- a deeply personal discipline.

When Starborn first arrived, they were seen with expected suspicion and hostility, but it was not long until they integrated into societies. Commonly, in fields of high intellectual demand, like arcane academia, and it’s no surprise that many became pioneers of astronomy.

The exact lifespan of a Starborn is unknown. They are not immortal \- eventually, starborns are recorded to die from old age; some, however, still remain pristinely functional after millenia. Perhaps that is one reason for their eternal patience and dedication to whatever it is that draws them, even as the natural currents of the hostile natural realm will taint and scar them.

Though their minds may be curious, their bodies are not. Starborn cannot easily reproduce, so many have forms without the attributed organs, or simply lack the desire to. For some, it’s a lifelong tragedy, a celestial condemnation of the species. For others, it’s the most liberating law of being starborn. No duty to an heir, no pull to lust, and none of the strings that come with that. They retain their beauty, their youth, for nothing more than enjoyment and expression.

Regardless, a starborn is not a flawless being. To be truly human, comes with its compromises. It comes with mortal flesh and mortal pains. Perhaps in some ancient, before-time contract, starborn could choose this brutal path, or not, and become a seek instead. Though, that’s simply a legend passed from the old stars.

### **Psionics and Celestial Magic**

Starborns and Seek alike are capable of Psionics, which does not necessarily replace their magical or martial aptitude. Their psionics are what enables them to traverse, comprehend, and interact with the world beyond their limbs. How they generate the psionic energy required is unknown; though, it is theorized that it comes from the resonance between crystal blades in their interior. 

For others, however, they instead come innately equipped with celestial magic, which they can cast simply with their essence. Oftentimes, this comes in the form of radiant light, which can be morphed and shaped to create a variety of different effects.

## **The seek**

The seek are, in many regards, nearly identical to their starborn cousins. The most telling sign is their eyes, which are often solid, stygian blacks. They are sometimes nicknamed as "night watchers" for this reason.

The appearance of the seek can best be described as...perfectly average. Whilst starborn may still hold personal flair and distinction in their appearances, not a single seek ever will. They have no need for beauty, expression or identity. Seek have no further use for the husk once its physical functions decline; For that reason, it aggressively optimises the energy expenditure within the body. Though some starborn lack reproductive organs entirely, all seek do; their legacies ending with them on their (very far) deathbed.

However, their bodies are not nearly as...detailed, as a normal humanoid mortal. They appear in many ways, a template \- the basic functions are all present, but devoid of all personal uniqueness. Perhaps that is the most telling sign of a seek's biology: The way they differ from the normal starborn is in their mind.

For a seek, the mind is the only thing that matters, and the rest is simply supplementary. Their minds are beyond advanced, holding perfect memory, advanced reasoning, rapid comprehension, and flawless arithmetic. Their minds are heightened to where each Seek is capable of psionics, which acts as both their main offensive and defensive measure. They utilize psionics seemingly without effort, capable of a wide array of telekinetic and telepathic measures. 

The origin story for these creatures are unknown, though the leading academic theory is that they are quantum cognition units, sent from some celestial force or origin to synthesize and gather data across the cosmos. When queried, even most seek do not know of their purpose, though they do not seem fettered or disturbed about it. 

The reason for that is a part of the seek’s core identity. Seek are exceptionally still and silent, seeming to lack impulse, irrationality, or compulsion; For many, they lack emotion entirely, operating solely on whatever their logic dictates. However, they can still develop cognitive empathy, compassion, or respect as much as they can manipulate, exploit, or deceive. Their brain is an emulation, though the efficacy of that emulation is determined through their mental stats, and isn't impervious to magical intrusion. 

The true, biological utility and evolutionary purpose of a seek is truly unknown, for they do not seem to hold any contempt for reproduction or socialisation. They are focused on no more than whatever enigma turns gears within their mind. Some Seek remain outsiders, on the edge of civilization, for millenia. Truthfully, why they come to land on any planet is unknown, though the leading theory is because they come to collect and synthesize data concerning it. 

The way they act is supposed to be as rational and as logical as they can be, and yet, don’t seem to appear that way at times. Seek seem to interweave with societies as they wish, leading masses to generosity and improvement, or waging war and devastation. Whatever their ulterior goals may be, they lack the remorse to feel any guilt from it. 

Finally, the relationship between a seek and a starborn is never frictionless. The two ultimately hold such different neurological designs, they are never able to understand the other. 

### **From seek to starborn**

As part of that, not all seek are perfect emulations. Again, the line between a seek and a normal starborn is blurry at best. Some seek may hold an inkling of space in their logical minds for illogical emotion. For many, it's a deficit \- it simply means they feel nothing but existential loneliness at their pointless existence. But for a few of these few \- will be flawed enough to feel. deeply, richly, as much as they think. Anomalies, they're called, being washed over with anger, grief, awe \- with no words to describe it. 

They will question them, grapple with them, test them. Starborn of this rarity are considered the most anomalous, but perhaps they are the most flawless emulation of the real human brain, with all of its flaws intentional. What does it mean to be noble when sacrifice is illogical? Why does love ache the heart? Why preserve honor for you in the present, and a legacy of you for the future? What is beauty? Art? 

and once a seek crosses the threshold \- there's often no way back. No way to go but forward, deeper into the dark ocean of unpredictable, yet endlessly deep emotion. For some starborn, this is seen as a reclaiming of a lost brethren. But to these seek, perhaps they have become something more than just that.

### **On the half-starborns**

Starborns and Seeks don't always have the luxury of a full humanoid form. Sometimes, if their stellar raw matter is compromised, budding starborn may instead meld into whatever humanoid stumbles across them, occupying a section of their mind to compute and utilising the physical husk for sustenance. An unwanted, but mandatory symbiosis is formed between the two.

These creatures don't always exhibit their starborn dwellers. Many of which appear perfectly normal, with the starborn solely residing inside them. and indeed, for some of these individuals, the stellar occupation isn't entirely unwelcome; an array of stellar magic and psionics are undeniable benefits. Some become acclaimed researchers or spellcasters.

Unfortunately, for the unlucky few, the stellar matter will fight their flesh, creating uncanny, or even downright horrific half-humanoid, half-stellar amalgamations, known as amalgams. 

trying to evict a starborn dwelled within is near impossible; they interlink and integrate into the nervous system of the creature. For an amalgam, it's even worse, as trying to remove starborn flesh is as painful as their own. The only known methods are reincarnation into an entirely different body, or through a dangerous, psionic incision, which starborn occupants are especially sensitive to.

Though, a starborn dweller isn't inherently malicious, and often, has no intention to be. They may coexist peacefully depending on what specific variation of merge between the two occurs, or be stuck in an eternal loop of two people in one body.

## arcana interactions

arcana is the measurement of not energy, but force. arcana governs the fundamental forces that tug at the fabric of spacetime. From magnetism to gravity, arcana handles the powers of forces across the cosmos. 

due to its fundamental nature, it is capable of acting alone, or interacting with other power sources to mold and manipulate other forms of energy.

#### subtype: Miracula
*Miracula* is a term coined by multiple arcane academia, and is the most proud innovation of past mages. Miracula is arcana, in its purest form, where a perfect lattice structure is formed. The weave is stablilised into a tangible, material form, yet preserves its magical density. 
It is nigh indestructable by physical force or energy, expressed in spells that form temporary globes of invunerability, walls of force, and many more new and radical inventions.  The material of which these spells manifest is known as *miracle matter* - perhaps the greatest and yet most misunderstood innovation of magic.

\column
#### subtype: Anasea
*anasea*, unlike miracula, is a runic term, not a modern one. in translation, it refers to the power of the primordial star. When Lumimnata is suffused by Arcana, we see the primordial light transform into something more pure - but also significantly more dangerous. Anasea manifests as *radiant* damage, in which this concentrated form of energy can beam across the cosmos at speeds faster than we can comprehend. 
 Radiant damage is one of the few methods of dismantling the coherence of miracle matter, as it scours and evicerates nearly anything without discrimination. Some say this was the first original power of divinity, before it was filtered down for their followers.
